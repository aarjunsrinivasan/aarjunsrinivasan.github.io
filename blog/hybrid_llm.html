Hybrid LMs at 32k, Extended: FlashAttention-3, Head-Dim Alignment, and a Sharper Comparison

My accepted Parameter Golf submission showed a Gated DeltaNet hybrid beating full attention at 32k context under a fixed 600-second budget. I then extended the study on an H100: FlashAttention-3 on both models, a schedule tied to wall-clock time, and a head-layout fix worth 15% of the hybrid's step time. The hybrid won every version of the comparison.

Apr 2026, extended Oct 2026 7 min read Long-context LLMs, efficient training, hybrid sequence mixers

OpenAI's Parameter Golf challenge asks for the best language model that fits in a 16 MB file after 600 seconds of training, scored on how well it compresses held-out web text (bits per byte, lower is better). I used the non-record track to test a question at the intersection of architecture and GPU efficiency: OLMo Hybrid replaces most attention layers with the cheaper recurrent Gated DeltaNet (GDN) — does that trade pay off in a 17M-parameter model at long context? The April study, accepted as a submission, said yes from 16k context up. The October extension confirmed it against a much stronger baseline and uncovered a kernel-alignment win on the hybrid's side.

Results in brief. At 32k context, 600 seconds on one H100, every model under 16 MB:

  • April (accepted submission): GDN hybrid beats full attention by 0.235 bpb (1.4709 vs 1.7059).
  • October, FlashAttention-3 on both models: the baseline's attention gets 1.76× faster and the hybrid still wins, by 0.065 bpb.
  • October, GDN head dim aligned 42 → 64: steps get 15% faster and the lead grows to 0.092 bpb (1.3908 vs 1.4829, two seeds each).

The question and the models

When context length grows, should a small model spend its budget on full quadratic attention everywhere, or should most layers use a cheaper recurrent mixer while a few layers preserve full attention?

The experiment compares two 9-layer models of similar size. The hybrid keeps full causal attention at layers 3 and 7 and uses a GDN mixer everywhere else, implemented with flash-linear-attention's chunk_gated_delta_rule; its width drops from 512 to 448 to stay in the same parameter regime.

Model Mixer layout Width Parameters
Full-attention baseline 9 attention layers 512 17.06M
GDN hybrid 7 GDN layers + 2 full-attention layers 448 17.42M

Both share the same recipe (RoPE, RMSNorm, GQA, Muon for matrix parameters, tied embeddings) and the competition's scoring: final_int8_zlib_roundtrip val_bpb on the full FineWeb validation split, after int8 quantization, zlib compression, and reload. Each run gets one H100 SXM and 600 seconds of wall-clock training, with warmup/warmdown schedules rescaled per model so the comparison is quality reached within a fixed time budget, not per-step quality.

April: full attention at 8k, the hybrid from 16k up

The trade-off changes sign exactly where the scaling argument predicts. At 8k, attention is cheap and the hybrid's recurrent layers don't pay for themselves (0.79× the baseline's step speed). At 16k the hybrid pulls ahead, and at 32k it runs 1.62× faster — about 62% more optimizer steps in the same 600 seconds — and wins decisively:

Lower val_bpb is better. 1x H100 SXM, 600 s wall-clock per run; the 32k hybrid number is a 2-seed mean. Code, logs and configs: PR #1371.

The 32k win was solid: stable across seeds (1.4703 and 1.4716), ahead of the baseline from the first validation checkpoint, and still improving when the baseline ran out of time. That crossover — not a single winning row — was the accepted result, and it set up the natural next experiment.

Extending the study: the strongest baseline I could build

The hybrid's 32k advantage was largely throughput, and the April baseline ran attention through PyTorch SDPA. A throughput-driven win is most informative when the baseline gets the fastest attention anyone would actually run, so I re-ran the 32k comparison with FlashAttention-3 on both models and warmup/warmdown set as fractions of the wall-clock budget. (Parameter Golf closed to new submissions over the summer, so the extension lives on my fork.) A reproduction run of the April hybrid on the new machine matched the original — same parameter count, matching early losses, 1.4630 vs 1.4703 — before anything changed.

FA3 helps the model that spends more time in attention: full attention speeds up 1.76× (2202 → 1253 ms/step), the hybrid 1.23×, since only 2 of its 9 layers are attention. The stronger baseline narrows the hybrid's lead to 0.065 bpb — a win that now stands against a kernel-fair opponent.

A head dimension the kernel actually uses

The best find was on the hybrid's own side. With width 448 and 8 heads, the GDN head dim works out to 42 — and the flash-linear-attention kernels pad that to 64 internally, so a third of every key lane was padding. Switching to 5 heads × 64 keeps nearly the same key/value width (320/640 vs 336/672), uses slightly fewer parameters, and makes each step 15% faster (1062 → 903 ms) with per-step learning curves within a few thousandths of the original. About 100 extra optimizer steps fit into the budget, and the lead over full attention grows to 0.092 bpb (1.3908 vs 1.4829).

Kimi Delta Attention at this scale

I also swapped GDN's one-decay-per-head gate for Kimi-Linear's per-channel decay (KDA), keeping everything else in the layer identical — after a numerical check that chunk_kda with a channel-constant gate reproduces chunk_gated_delta_rule. At 17M parameters and 600 seconds, the simpler gate wins: KDA matches GDN per step at best, and its kernel is 14–23% slower, which costs 85–105 steps. Net, KDA trails GDN by 0.045 bpb at 8×42 and 0.021 at 5×64.

What I take from it

In a 17M-parameter, 600-second, single-H100 training regime, full attention remains preferable at 8k context, but a GDN hybrid becomes preferable at 16k and above — under SDPA and under FlashAttention-3.

Two mechanisms drive it. Long-context full attention gets expensive quickly, so replacing 7 of 9 attention layers buys the hybrid more optimizer steps inside the same clock. And the recurrent state likely acts as a useful inductive bottleneck for a very small model: most layers compress history while two layers keep global access.

The broader lesson is the one the extension demonstrates directly: under a fixed time budget, architecture and GPU efficiency jointly determine the quality you can reach. A faster attention kernel narrowed the gap; a better head layout widened it again. Giving both models the same optimization attention — equal kernels, schedules tied to the budget, shapes checked against what the kernel actually computes — is what made the comparison informative.

What's next

Planned follow-ups: re-locate the 8k–16k crossover under FA3 (which should shift it, since FA3 helps full attention most at long context), run an SDPA arm under the wall-clock schedule to quantify the kernel's contribution on its own, sweep the attention-layer placement and the GDN-to-attention ratio, and add seeds at 16k. The extension so far covers 32k with two seeds per model.
